The Experience

Istanbul, usually seen
as a checklist —
read as one city instead

Most Old City tours move from monument to monument. This walk follows a different logic: the streets, public spaces, neighbourhoods and surviving traces that reveal how the city changed — from Constantinople to Ottoman Istanbul, and into the city that exists today.

"Reading Istanbul through the traces its many lives have left behind."

Begin inside Topkapı Palace's First Courtyard, then move from the monumental center around Hagia Sophia and the Hippodrome into Kadırga's neighbourhood streets — passing a Greek Orthodox church, an Ottoman hammam, and the route of the ancient Mese and the Column of Constantine, before ending at the Grand Bazaar and Beyazıt Square. Most stops along the way are viewed from the street; a few offer optional interior access depending on the day.

No paid admission is required for the standard itinerary. Just four to five hours on foot.

The Hippodrome — Constantinople's ceremonial and civic heart

What You'll See

From an imperial gate
to a merchant's threshold.

01
Ottoman · Imperial

Topkapı's Imperial Gate

Sultans entered through this gate. We start where they did — inside the palace's free First Courtyard, no palace ticket required, alongside Hagia Irene, an Eastern Roman church the Ottomans never converted.

02
Roman · Civic

The Hippodrome

Once Constantinople's ceremonial heart. Its surviving monuments tell more than one story — objects moved here, altered, stripped of their gilding by the Fourth Crusade, and sometimes carried away entirely.

03
Ottoman · Neighbourhood

Kadırga's Streets

We leave the monumental center for a different scale of city life — a Sinan-built mosque fitted into sloping terrain, the neighbourhood around a still-active Greek Orthodox church (interior access depends on opening conditions), and an Ottoman neighbourhood hammam viewed from outside.

04
Roman to Modern

Grand Bazaar & Beyazıt Square

We walk through the Grand Bazaar as a living commercial district, not a shopping stop, and emerge at Beyazıt Square — built on the ground of the ancient Forum of Theodosius.
